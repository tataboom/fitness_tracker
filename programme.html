Read this first

Four things that decide whether this works.

You cannot choose where the fat comes off

No exercise burns fat from the area it works. Crunches do not flatten a stomach and bench press does not clear a chest. Fat leaves the whole body at once, in an order your genetics set. For many men the belly and flanks are among the last to change, and the chest varies from person to person. Expect your face, arms and legs to change first. That is normal, not failure.

Soft chest fat and a firm disc are different things

Soft tissue spreading toward the armpit is fat, and it goes when you lose fat. A firm, rubbery disc directly behind the nipple, sometimes tender or on one side only, is glandular tissue, not fat, and it does not respond to training or dieting at any bodyweight. If you can feel a distinct disc there, raise it with your GP now rather than after a year of work. It is common and treatable, and knowing which you have tells you what to expect.

The kitchen removes the layer, the bar builds what is under it

Pressing builds the muscle beneath the fat, which changes the shape of the chest. It does not remove the layer over it. Both have to happen, from two different places: the deficit does the first, the bar does the second.

This is why the iron matters for fat loss

In a deficit you lose fat and muscle together. Training hard against a load is the signal that tells your body to hold the muscle and take the difference from fat instead. That is the whole job of the equipment: it decides whether you end up slim and solid, or a smaller version of the same shape.

Sessions run 55 to 70 minutes. The logbook eases each session type in over its first two outings (2 sets, then 3, then full), so training early never forces a light week later. If a week goes wrong, drop Lower B first and carry on where you left off.

Warm-up, every session

Five minutes. Never logged.

  • Two minutes of easy movement: arm circles, hip hinges, bodyweight squats, a few press-ups.
  • On the first big lift only: the empty bar for 10, then about half your working weight for 6, then three-quarters for 3.
  • Then start your first working set. Warm-up sets do not go in the logbook, only working sets do.

The sessions

In order. The first two lifts are the ones that matter.

Upper A

horizontal push & pull
  1. Barbell bench press4 × 6–8 · 2–3 min

    Read the solo-bench protocol below before your first session.

  2. Barbell bent-over row4 × 8–10 · 2 min

    Hinge to about 45 degrees, pull to the bottom of the ribs. Match your bench weight over time.

  3. Incline dumbbell press3 × 10–12 · 90 s
  4. Inverted row3 × max · 90 s

    Bar in the stands at 110 cm, check both cups are seated, pull your chest to it. Your pull-up substitute.

  5. Dumbbell lateral raise3 × 12–15 · 60 s
  6. Preacher curl3 × 10–12 · 60 s
  7. Overhead dumbbell extension3 × 10–12 · 60 s

    Kinder on the shoulders than the old bench dip, and it hits the triceps through a full stretch.

Lower A

squat pattern & core
  1. Bulgarian split squat3 × 8–10 / leg · 2 min

    First now, while you are fresh, with a dumbbell in each hand. The best leg exercise you have without a rack. Start bodyweight only.

  2. Goblet squat4 × 12–15 · 90 s

    Higher reps here: a single dumbbell runs out of load first, so use it for volume, not for a max.

  3. Dumbbell Romanian deadlift4 × 10–12 · 90 s
  4. Step-up onto bench3 × 10 / leg · 60 s
  5. Barbell hip thrust3 × 12–15 · 90 s

    Shoulder blades on the bench edge, towel under the bar until you buy a pad.

  6. Standing calf raise4 × 15–20 · 45 s
  7. Plank3 × 30–60 s · 45 s

Upper B

vertical push & pull
  1. Standing overhead press4 × 6–8 · 2–3 min

    Quarter-squat to drive the bar out of the stands, step back, press. If re-racking feels shaky, lower to the floor.

  2. Single-arm dumbbell row4 × 10–12 / side · 90 s

    Heaviest single dumbbell you can build. This is the lift that will want the plates.

  3. Flat dumbbell press3 × 10–12 · 90 s

    The safe way to press to failure: you can drop these to your sides. Use this, not the barbell, to find your limit.

  4. Dumbbell pullover3 × 12–15 · 60 s
  5. Inverted row, feet elevated3 × max · 90 s

    Feet on a chair to make it harder than Monday. Progress by elevation, not weight.

  6. Hammer curl3 × 12 · 60 s
  7. Lying triceps extension3 × 10–12 · 60 s

    Bar to the forehead, elbows still, light. Keep it away from your face by starting the bar slightly behind your head.

Lower B

hinge pattern & carries
  1. Barbell Romanian deadlift4 × 8–10 · 2–3 min

    Your plates sit about 13 cm off the floor, so the first rep is a deadlift: stand it up, brace, then hinge. Heaviest lift of the week.

  2. Reverse lunge3 × 10 / leg · 90 s
  3. Single-leg glute bridge3 × 12 / side · 60 s
  4. Goblet squat, 3-second lower3 × 12 · 90 s
  5. Farmer’s carry4 × 40 m · 60 s

    Heaviest dumbbells you can build, walk until the grip fails. Conditioning, core and grip with no joint impact.

  6. Dead bug3 × 10 / side · 45 s

The barbell good morning was cut: it duplicated the two Romanian deadlifts and carried the most risk for the least return of anything in the plan.

The solo bench protocol

No catches, no spotter. Read once, follow every time.

How not to get pinned

  • Set the stands high. You should lift the bar off with your arms almost straight, then lower it to your chest. If you have to press it up out of the stands, they are too low.
  • No collars on the bench. A failed rep has to be able to tip and let the plates slide off one end. Practise that escape once with the empty bar so it is not new when you need it.
  • Leave one in the tank. Rep 8 is never a grind. If a rep would be a maximal fight, that was your last rep. Racking is for when you have finished, not when you have failed.
  • Find your limit on dumbbells, not the bar. The flat dumbbell press in Upper B is where you push to true failure, because you can drop the weights to your sides.

When the squat rack with safety arms arrives next year, this all gets easier and you can bench heavier with a clear head.

Progression

The part that stops it being boring.

Visible progress keeps training interesting, not novelty. The exercises stay put for twelve weeks so you can actually see yourself getting stronger.

Double progression. Every lift has a rep range. Pick a load you can hit the bottom of the range with, then add reps week to week on the same weight. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add the smallest jump and drop back to the bottom. The logbook does this maths for you and offers the jump with one tap.

When a lift stalls. Three sessions stuck at the same weight and reps means back off: drop the weight about 10 percent and build back up. A stall is information, not failure.

Week 8 is a deload. It lands near Christmas. Cut every working set to 2 and keep the weights light. You will come back stronger, not weaker.

End of week 12. Take stock: what went up, what stalled, how the mirror and the tape look. That review writes block two, usually the same shape with the lifts that stalled swapped or re-approached. By then the squat rack should be close.

Eating, briefly

General guidance, not a prescription.

The training decides what you keep. The food decides what you lose.

  • Protein, about 150 g a day. The one number most people miss. It protects muscle in a deficit. Spread it across the day.
  • Maintenance is roughly 2,700 to 2,900 kcal for you. Start eating near 2,300, which is a moderate deficit, and hold it steady.
  • Let the scale steer, not the calculator. Under 0.3 kg a week averaged over three weeks: cut another 200. Over 1 kg a week: add 200 back, you are losing it too fast to hold muscle.
  • 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day. At 100 kg this outweighs any cardio machine and costs your knees nothing. Malta’s promenades are built for it.

Honest timeline: 100 kg to around 82 kg is about eight to twelve months. It is the same months either way.

Track four things

The logbook does the rest.

  • Load and reps for every working set. Non-negotiable. Progression does not work from memory.
  • Bodyweight every morning, same conditions. Read only the weekly average. Daily swings of 1 to 2 kg are water.
  • Waist at the navel, once a week. It keeps moving when the scale stalls, and for your goal it is the more honest number.
  • A photo every four weeks, same light and spot. You will not see day-to-day change. You will see it across three photos.
